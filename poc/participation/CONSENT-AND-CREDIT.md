## Mashtots: helping a computer read Armenian

**What the project is.** Mashtots is a research and art project that studies how computer language models read Armenian, Eastern, Western and Classical, and builds small
models and tools that read it better. It is run by ____ (the project lead) with ____ (partners). Contact: ____.

**What we ask of you.** To read short Armenian sentences and say which Armenian they are and how they are spelled, and to cut some words into their parts, using a form
that runs on your own computer. A batch takes about ____ minutes. You can stop at any time.

**What we collect.**
* Your answers to the tasks, with a random code the form makes (for example `anon-3f9a01c2`). The code is not linked to your name in the answer files.
* On this form only, and only if you wish: the name you want to be credited under, and how to reach you about the project. This form is kept separately from your
  answers, by ____, and is never published or shared with anyone outside the project team.
* We do **not** collect your age, address, or anything else about you. Please do not write anything about yourself in the note field.

**How your work is used.** To measure and improve how the models handle Western and Eastern Armenian; in research papers and reports; in the project's demonstration and
art piece. Your answers become part of a labelled data set released under the licence chosen by the governance group (see below; you will be told which before release).

**Credit.** Choose one:
- [ ] Credit me by name as: ____________________ (on the project page, in the data set's list of contributors, and in the art piece's credits)
- [ ] Credit me by first name and town / community only: ____________________
- [ ] Credit my school / parish / organisation: ____________________
- [ ] Do not name me (anonymous contribution)

Scholars who adjudicate and contribute substantially to the gold standard will be offered co-authorship of the data set paper.

**Your rights.** You can withdraw at any time. Before a data set is released, withdrawing removes your answers from it; after a release, we remove them from every later
release and from models trained afterwards (a model already published cannot be "un-trained"; we will say so honestly). You can ask what we hold about you (only this form).

**Children and schools.** Pupils take part only through their school, with the teacher in charge and the school's own permission process (parental consent where the school
requires it). No pupil names or details are collected; the school or class is credited.

**Texts you bring.** If you offer texts (letters, newspapers, parish records, books), tell us who wrote them and who holds the rights. We only use texts whose rights holder
agrees, and we never use private letters or records that name living people without their consent.

## Licence options for contributed labels (for the governance group to decide; owner: "decide later")

| option | what it allows | fits with | community control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CC BY-SA 4.0** | anyone may use, adapt and share, with credit, under the same licence | Wikipedia and the UD treebanks (the same licence) | low: anyone may use, including large companies, with attribution |
| **CC BY 4.0** | the same without share-alike | most research data | low |
| **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** | non-commercial use only | UD CAVaL (the Grabar Gospels) | medium: commercial use needs permission; mixing with open data gets harder |
| **Community licence (CARE / Kaitiakitanga-style)** | the community board decides each use; commercial use needs its consent | data-sovereignty practice (Te Hiku Media, Local Contexts labels) | high: needs a standing board and a process |
| **CC0** | public domain | anything | none |

A common path: labels under CC BY-SA (they are about public text and help everyone), contributed *texts* under whatever their rights holders allow, and a community board that
decides on model releases. The board is described in `GOVERNANCE.md`.
